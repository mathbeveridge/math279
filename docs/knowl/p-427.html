Break the chocolate bar along the \(i\)th column to get two chocolate bars: one \(i \times s\) and one \((r-i) \times s\text{.}\) By strong induction, it takes \(is-1\) breaks to finish the left bar and it takes \((r-i)s -1\) breaks to finish the right one. The total number of breaks used is

\begin{equation*} 1 + (is-1) + ((r-i)s-1) = rs -1. \end{equation*}

Note that \(r\) and \(s\) reference the dimensions of the chocolate bar, given \(n=rs\text{.}\)
